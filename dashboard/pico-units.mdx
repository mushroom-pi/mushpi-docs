The **Pico Units** screen is where you manage the units themselves — one Pico per grow space. It has two levels: the list, which shows every unit, and a unit's own page, reached by clicking a unit.

## The units list

The list shows one card per unit, in a grid, with pagination when you have enough of them. Each card shows:

- The unit's **name** and **handle**, and its **face colour**.
- A **health badge** — healthy, degraded, offline, or unmonitored.
- **Last seen** — when the unit last reported.
- Its **host** address, and a **firmware / API-generation** caption.
- A **monitored** switch (display only — you change it on the unit's own page).

A **Refresh** icon on each card re-reads that unit, and the list itself can be refetched. A unit that is offline is dimmed, and its card shows a **Reconnect** button, which re-runs the connect flow for that unit.

## Connect a new Pico

**Connect new Pico** opens a four-step wizard that discovers and provisions a unit: **Diagnose**, **Connect**, **Setup**, **Finish**. You read the unit's LED to pick the right path, join the `mushpi-provision-…` Wi-Fi network the unit broadcasts, open the provisioning page, and the wizard then watches for the new unit to appear in the list.

If the wizard cannot find the unit, you can **Register Manually** instead, which asks only for the unit's **handle**. The handle is how a unit identifies itself when it announces to the hub — the mechanics are covered in [Register a Pico unit](/deployment/pico-registration), and the Wi-Fi provisioning the wizard walks you through is covered in [Provisioning a Pico unit](/hardware/provisioning). A manually registered unit does not wait for the next sweep: the hub polls it once immediately at creation, so its first reading and its network address are captured right away.

## A unit's own page

Click a unit to open its page. The top shows the unit's **name**, its health badge, its **handle**, its description, and a **firmware-compatibility badge** — the last of which appears only when the server flags the unit's API contract as incompatible.

From here you can:

- **Edit** the unit's **name**, **description**, **face colour**, and whether it is **monitored**. When monitoring is off, the server stops collecting readings for that unit — the Pico itself keeps running its control loop.
- **Reboot** the unit — a soft reboot (faster, keeps cumulative uptime) or a hard reboot (a full power-cycle).
- **Reconnect** the unit — shown only when it is offline.
- **Delete** the unit — permanent, and it removes the unit's readings and configuration too.

Below the header, the page lays out the unit's details in cards.

### Controls

The **Controls** card shows, and its dialog edits, the **target temperature**, the **target humidity**, and the **control-loop on/off toggle**. These targets are the numbers the control loop works toward — what the loop does with a target is explained in [How the control loop works](/configuration/hysteresis-control). With the loop on, the unit switches devices automatically to meet the targets; with it off, the devices have to be switched by hand.

### Devices

The **Devices** card shows whether the **humidifier**, **fan** and **heater** are currently on or off. Its dialog lets you switch them by hand, but only while the control loop is **off** — while the loop is on, the unit controls them and the switches are locked. A manual override is temporary: the moment you turn the loop back on, it takes over again on its own schedule and your hand-set positions no longer apply.

### PIN mapping

The mapping card shows which **GPIO pin** each device uses — the DHT11 sensor, humidifier, fan and heater. The **PIN Mapping** dialog lets you change those assignments, behind a caution and a confirmation that you have verified your wiring, since a wrong pin can drive the wrong device.

### Batches

The unit's own **Batches** card lists the batches that have run on this unit, with the active one marked, and a button to start a new batch directly on it.
